3. End-to-End Workflow

The core business process observed across the CRM, Contract, Scheduling, and Billing screens:

3.1 Narrative walk-through

A Lead enters the CRM with source (Website, Google Ads, Google Business Profile, WhatsApp, Call, Referral), urgency, and property type; leads pass through statuses New → Interested → Converted (or Not Interested / Cancelled), with system-flagged Overdue Follow-up and Stale (3+ Days) states.

A Site Inspection can be recorded pre-quotation — "Record a pre-quotation site survey, then build a quotation from it" — capturing areas, technician, and date.

A Quotation is built with a Lead/Existing-Customer picker, a separate Service Address, and a Pricing & Tax block: GST vs No-GST toggle, CGST+SGST vs IGST toggle, GST % (default 18), and an Amount-GST-Exclusive/Inclusive switch. Services are added as line rows, each computing its own visits and total. Status flow: Draft → Pending Approval → Accepted/Rejected → Converted.

An accepted Quotation converts into a Contract (AMC), which auto-generates a visit schedule surfaced on the Service Calendar (color-coded by status: Scheduled, Assigned, In Progress, Completed, Cancelled, Rescheduled) and in Smart Scheduling (List / Calendar / Board / Technicians views, filterable by technician, city, service, payment status).

Field Operations assigns technicians to each visit (Assign Services screen: Customer & Site → Service & Assignment → Service Details, with GPS lat/long, access instructions, work start date/time).

Technicians close the Job Card in the field (captured via the mobile app per the platform's stated feature set: chemicals used, before/after photos, digital signature); this is the billable trigger.

Billing staff raise an Invoice from the Invoices module (list view shows Total Invoices, Invoiced amount, Paid Amount, Due Amount, filterable by Status/Payment/date range).

Payments are recorded through Accounting → Receipt Voucher and reconciled against invoices; Payment Voucher and Expenses handle the outward side; the Ledger consolidates both.

Customer Outstandings and the dashboard's Receivables Aging widget report 0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 90+ day buckets; Payment Reminders push WhatsApp/email nudges on a configurable cadence.

GST Reports, Financial Reports, and Business Reports (under Reports & Analytics) consume the same invoice/payment data for statutory filing and management visibility.

Complaints can optionally link to a Contract and are assigned to technicians independently of the billing flow, but roll into the Pest Control Dashboard's Complaint Breakdown and Performance & Quality widgets.

8. Non-Functional Requirements

Category Requirement
Compliance GST invoice fields (GSTIN, HSN/SAC, tax breakup, sequential number, place of supply) mandatory on every GST-treatment invoice; No-GST companies use a separate numbering series.
Data integrity A customer must have an active Contract before services can be assigned (observed constraint on the Assign Services screen) — enforce equivalently at the API layer.
Auditability Invoices are never hard-deleted; status changes and voids are logged.
Access control Per-employee App Access and Web Access flags, plus a Permission Role, as seen on the Employees screen.
Offline support Technician job-card completion should queue offline and sync, consistent with field-app expectations.
Reporting consistency Dashboard KPIs (Total Outstanding, Collection Efficiency, Receivables Aging) must derive from the same ledger data as the Reports & Analytics module — no parallel calculation paths.
Localization ₹ (INR) formatting, dd-mm-yyyy date inputs (as seen throughout), Indian state list for place-of-supply logic.

10. Current Implementation Assessment

This section reconciles the intended PestVyapar product with the implementation present in the Tech-house-pest-control workspace as reviewed on 26 August 2026.

Implemented in the current prototype

• React 19 and Vite single-page interface with ten operational views: Dashboard, CRM, Lead Pipeline, Service Calendar, Quotations, Invoices, General Ledger, Inventory, Complaints, and People/Attendance.
• Express and MongoDB/Mongoose API covering leads, customers, quotations, contracts, visits, invoices, receipts, expenses, ledgers, products, stock movements, technicians, employees, vendors, attendance, and complaints.
• Lead status movement and conversion into a customer record.
• Quotation creation with GST treatment, inclusive/exclusive pricing, service rates, validity dates, and visit counts.
• Quotation-to-AMC conversion that creates a contract and recurring visit records.
• Technician job completion with chemical consumption, signature evidence, inventory deduction, and stock movement logging.
• Visit-linked invoicing, GST calculations, customer receivables, FIFO payment allocation, and general-ledger entries.
• Seeded demonstration data and responsive dashboard summaries.

Important gaps before production

• No authentication, password management, session handling, tenant isolation, or enforced role-based authorization.
• No automated test suite, CI/CD pipeline, API documentation, schema migrations, monitoring, backup verification, or disaster-recovery procedure.
• No real technician mobile application, offline synchronization, push notifications, GPS collection, route optimization, photo upload storage, or biometric attendance.
• No payment gateway, WhatsApp Business API, email/SMS provider, GST/e-invoice integration, maps provider, or accounting export integration.
• Accounting is a simplified operational ledger, not yet a fully reconciled double-entry accounting and statutory compliance system.
• Several displayed values and defaults are demonstration assumptions, including fixed visit billing, default technician assignment, invoice numbering, and seeded dates.
• The seed endpoint deletes collections and must be disabled or tightly restricted outside development.

12. Detailed Operational Workflows

12.1 Lead-to-Contract

1. Capture inquiry from web, phone, referral, campaign, import, or WhatsApp. 2. Validate contact details and detect duplicates. 3. Assign owner, urgency, pest type, property, and follow-up SLA. 4. Conduct remote qualification or site inspection. 5. Record infestation, measurements, risks, images, recommended treatment, and chemical plan. 6. Generate a versioned quotation with taxes, terms, visit frequency, exclusions, and validity. 7. Send through email/WhatsApp and record delivery. 8. Capture approval, rejection, negotiation, or expiry. 9. Convert approval into customer/site, AMC or one-time contract, billing plan, and service schedule.

12.2 Contract-to-Service

1. Generate visits from service frequency and blackout rules. 2. Validate technician skill, certification, shift, branch, stock, travel time, and capacity. 3. Assign and notify technician and customer. 4. Technician checks in with time and GPS evidence. 5. Complete safety checklist, observations, treatment, chemical batch/quantity, photos, recommendations, and customer signature. 6. Capture exceptions such as no access, customer unavailable, unsafe site, revisit required, or partial completion. 7. Supervisor reviews exceptions and closes or reschedules the visit. 8. Create service report/certificate and notify stakeholders.

12.3 Service-to-Cash

1. Determine invoice trigger: advance, milestone, visit, monthly, or contract completion. 2. Build tax-compliant invoice from approved contract and completed work. 3. Apply GST place-of-supply rules, discounts, TDS, credit notes, and rounding. 4. Deliver invoice with payment link. 5. Run reminder cadence based on due date and customer preferences. 6. Record receipt and allocate it to invoices. 7. Reconcile gateway/bank transactions and resolve short or excess payments. 8. Update customer ledger, aging, collection forecast, and branch revenue.

12.4 Inventory-to-Consumption

1. Maintain product, chemical, unit, batch, expiry, safety data, supplier, and reorder settings. 2. Raise purchase request/order and record goods receipt. 3. Issue material to technician or vehicle. 4. Record actual job consumption by batch. 5. Return unused stock and quarantine damaged/expired stock. 6. Reconcile book versus physical stock. 7. Alert on low stock, unusual usage, expiry, and restricted chemicals. 8. Calculate job-level material cost and margin.

12.5 Complaint-to-Resolution

1. Log complaint against customer, property, contract, and prior visit. 2. Classify severity, recurrence, safety risk, warranty coverage, and SLA. 3. Assign investigation or revisit. 4. Capture root cause, corrective action, evidence, and consumed material. 5. Obtain customer acknowledgement and satisfaction score. 6. Escalate overdue or repeated cases. 7. Feed patterns into technician coaching, pest trends, and service-design improvement.

13. Recommended Technical Architecture

• Frontend: modular React application with TypeScript, routing, accessible component library, query caching, validation, error boundaries, and role-aware navigation.
• Backend: versioned REST API organized by domain services; request validation; centralized errors; idempotency for payments/webhooks; transaction boundaries for stock, invoices, and receipts.
• Data: MongoDB may remain suitable for operational documents, but accounting entries should use immutable journal rules and transactions; add indexes, tenantId and branchId to every scoped record.
• Mobile: React Native or Flutter technician app with encrypted offline queue, conflict rules, background location consent, media compression, and resumable upload.
• Infrastructure: managed database, object storage for media/documents, queue workers, cache, secrets manager, CDN/WAF, centralized logs, metrics, alerts, and tested backups.
• Integrations: provider adapters for WhatsApp, email/SMS, maps, payment gateways, GST/e-invoicing, and exports to common accounting platforms.
• Security: MFA for privileged roles, short-lived sessions, rate limits, audit trail, secure headers, encryption, malware scanning for uploads, field-level access, and periodic penetration testing.

16. Pros and Cons

Pros

• Strong industry fit: sales, AMC operations, field evidence, chemicals, billing, and renewals are connected in one domain model.
• The prototype already demonstrates the core lead-to-cash story and cross-module automation.
• GST-aware billing, recurring visits, inventory deduction, complaints, and attendance create more value than a generic CRM alone.
• Unlimited-user positioning can encourage whole-team adoption and reduce spreadsheet fragmentation.
• A unified dataset enables customer profitability, technician productivity, renewal, and pest-trend insights.

Cons and risks

• The current prototype is not production-ready because security, tenant isolation, tests, monitoring, and operational controls are absent.
• The proposed scope is ERP-sized; attempting every module simultaneously creates schedule, quality, and accounting-compliance risk.
• Field reliability depends on a genuine offline-first mobile experience, robust media synchronization, location permissions, and device support.
• Accounting, GST, payroll, AI audit, and routing claims require specialist validation and ongoing regulatory/provider maintenance.
• Large all-in-one navigation can overwhelm small operators unless onboarding, presets, progressive disclosure, and role-specific workspaces are provided.
• WhatsApp, maps, storage, messaging, and payment services introduce usage costs that may conflict with a very low flat subscription price.
• Unlimited users without tenant quotas and abuse controls can create unpredictable infrastructure and support costs.

20. Definition of Production Readiness
