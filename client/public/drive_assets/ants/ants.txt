ANT
Ants undergo complete metamorphosis (holometabolous development), passing through four distinct life stages: Egg, Larva, Pupa, and Adult. The total transition typically takes anywhere from a few weeks to several months depending on species and temperature.
The Four Stages of the Ant Life Cycle
1. Egg StageAppearance: Tiny, oval, soft-shelled, and usually smooth or white/translucent.
Process: The queen lays thousands of tiny eggs throughout her lifetime. Unfertilized eggs develop into male drones, while fertilized eggs hatch into female ants (workers or future queens).
2. Larva StageAppearance: Worm-like, legless, grub-shaped larvae.
Process: After hatching, the larva grows rapidly by shedding its skin multiple times (molting). Because adult ants cannot consume solid food easily, worker ants feed the larvae liquid regurgitated food or solid particles that larvae chew using specialized mouthparts.
3. Pupa StageAppearance: Pale, motionless forms resembles adult ants with their legs and antennae folded against their bodies. In some species, larvae spin a silken cocoon around themselves before pupating.
Process: This is the transformation phase where the ant's internal structures reorganize into adult legs, eyes, and body segments.
4. Adult StageAppearance: Fully developed ant with a hardened exoskeleton.
Roles in the Colony:Queen: Reproductive female that lays all colony eggs; lives the longest.
Workers: Sterile females responsible for foraging, caring for brood, defending the nest, and building tunnels.
Drones (Males): Winged males whose sole function is to mate with young queens during a nuptial flight.
How a New Colony Alone of Carpenter Ant Queen Starts?
A carpenter ant queen starts a new colony completely alone through a process called claustral founding. She seals herself in a chamber and raises her first generation of workers using only her body's stored reserves—without ever stepping outside to forage.
1.Mating Flight and Wing Shedding:Nuptial flight occurs in late spring or early summer.Winged virgin queens (alate females) leave their parent nest to mate with winged drones in mid-air. During this flight, the queen stores millions of sperm cells inside a specialized organ called a spermatheca, which lasts her whole lifetime (up to 20–25 years).
2.Excavating the Founding Chamber:Seals herself off completely.The queen searches for a secure, moist wooden structure—such as a rotting log, stump, or damp wall void. 
3.Laying the First Eggs:Tends the brood in total isolation.She lays a small clutch of 15–20 eggs. 
4.Raising 'Nanitic' Workers:First generation of miniaturized ants.The larvae develop into pupae and emerge as adult workers called nanitics. 
5.Handing Over Colony Duties:The queen becomes a full-time egg layer.The new nanitic workers break open the founding chamber to begin foraging outside, excavating larger galleries, and caring for subsequent broods.
