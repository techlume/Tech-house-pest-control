 COCKROACH
Cockroaches are among the most resilient insects on Earth, belonging to the order Blattodea. Out of more than 4,500 species worldwide, only about 30 are considered household pests.
The Cockroach Life Cycle
 
Cockroaches undergo incomplete metamorphosis (paurometabolous development), which consists of three distinct stages: Egg, Nymph, and Adult.
Egg (Ootheca)
Female cockroaches produce protective purse-shaped egg cases called oothecae.
 
Depending on the species, an ootheca can contain anywhere from 10 to 50 eggs.
Females either carry the egg case until hatching (like the German cockroach) or deposit it in a hidden, moist area.
Nymph
Newly hatched nymphs emerge white or pale before their exoskeleton hardens and darkens.
Nymphs resemble smaller, wingless adults.
 
As they grow, they shed their hard outer skin (molting) multiple times (typically 6 to 14 instars/stages) before reaching maturity.
Adult
After the final molt, the cockroach reaches adulthood, fully developing functional wings (in most species) and reproductive capabilities.
 
Adults can live anywhere from a few months to over two years depending on species and environmental conditions.

Wood cockroach
The wood cockroach (primarily referring to the Parcoblatta species, such as the Pennsylvania wood cockroach) is an outdoor-dwelling insect native to forested areas. Unlike typical household pests, wood cockroaches rarely infest indoor spaces because they rely on outdoor moisture and decaying plant matter to survive.
 
Key Identification FeaturesAppearance: Chestnut brown to dark brown body with distinct light, semi-translucent margins along the outer edges of the thorax and wings.
Size: Adults measure around 12 to 25 mm (0.5 to 1 inch) long.
Sexual Dimorphism: Males have long, fully developed wings extending past their abdomen and are active fliers. Females have short, underdeveloped wings (pad-like) and cannot fly.
Behavior: Unlike light-avoiding household roaches, male wood cockroaches are strongly attracted to bright outdoor lights and will fly toward them at night.
Signs of an Infestation
Droppings: Small species like German cockroaches leave droppings that look like coffee grounds or black pepper on countertops and in drawers. Larger species leave cylindrical, ridge-like droppings.
Smell: A persistent, oily, or musty odor produced by cockroach pheromones that intensifies as the population grows.
 
Egg Cases (Oothecae): Dark, brownish, capsule-shaped egg cases left behind in hidden, dark places like behind appliances, under sinks, or inside cabinets.
Shed Skins: Translucent, light-brown skins shed by growing nymphs during molting.
Smear Marks: Dark, irregular streaks along walls or baseboards where cockroaches crawl in areas with high moisture.
