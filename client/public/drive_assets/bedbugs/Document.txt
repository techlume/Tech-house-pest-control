BED BUGS
The life cycle of a bed bug (Cimex lectularius) consists of 7 distinct stages: an egg stage, 5 nymphal stages, and the adult stage.
Understanding these stages is essential for effective pest control, as treatment methods vary depending on the bug's current development phase.

1. The 7 Life Stages
 
Eggs, translucent nymphs, and adult bed bug. Source: Tech House Pest control
Egg: Tiny (~1 mm), pearly-white, and stuck to surfaces with a sticky coating. They hatch in roughly 6 to 10 days depending on temperature.
 
1st Instar Nymph: ~1.5 mm long. Nearly translucent or pale yellow before feeding. Must consume a blood meal to shed its exoskeleton (molt) into the next stage.
 
2nd Instar Nymph: ~2.0 mm long. Slightly darker and larger, but still difficult to spot without close inspection.
3rd Instar Nymph: ~2.5 mm long. Becomes increasingly visible on seams and mattress crevices.
4th Instar Nymph: ~3.0 mm long. Takes on a deeper amber tone as the exoskeleton thickens.
5th Instar Nymph: ~4.5 mm long. Reaches near-adult size, requiring one final blood meal before transforming into a sexually mature adult.
Adult: 5 to 7 mm (about the size of an apple seed). Flat, oval, reddish-brown body. After feeding, adults swell and turn a darker mahogany-red. Females can lay 200–500 eggs over their lifetime.
2. Why the Life Cycle Matters for Pest Control
Required Blood Meals: A nymph cannot advance to the next stage without taking at least one blood meal between each molt.

Egg Resistance to Sprays: Most contact chemical sprays kill adult bugs and nymphs but do not kill the eggs. This is why a follow-up treatment is usually scheduled 10 to 14 days later to kill newly hatched nymphs before they reach maturity.
Temperature & Heat Treatment: Bed bugs grow faster in warmer temperatures (around 70°F–80°F / 21°C–27°C). Professional heat treatments (raising ambient room temperatures above 120°F / 49°C for several hours) are effective because high heat destroys all life stages, including eggs, in a single session.
 
Unlike termites or carpenter ants, bed bugs do not chew wood, weaken structural foundations, or bore through drywalls. However, severe infestations cause significant secondary cosmetic, material, and financial damage to home interiors.
