SLIVER FISH
Silverfish (Lepisma saccharina) undergo ametabolous development (no metamorphosis). This means they develop without dramatic physical transformations—hatchlings emerge looking like miniature adults and simply grow larger through continuous molting.
 
The 3-stage life cycle of a silverfish
1. Primary Stages of Development
Stage 1: Egg (19–60 Days)
Deposition: Females deposit small clusters of 2 to 20 oval, whitish eggs in dark, protected crevices, baseboard gaps, or paper stacks.
Hatching Time: Incubation depends heavily on temperature and humidity
Warm, humid conditions Eggs hatch in 19 to 20 days.Cooler, drier conditions
 Hatching can take up to 60 days.
Stage 2: Nymph (3–4 Months)
Appearance: Newly hatched nymphs are translucent white, measuring under 2 mm long, and lack the signature metallic sheen.
 
Continuous Molting: As they feed on starches, sugars, and cellulose (paper, glue, wallpaper paste), nymphs shed their exoskeleton continuously.
Scale Development: Around the 3rd or 4th molt, they develop protective silvery-gray scales and characteristic antenna length.
Stage 3: Adult (Lifespan: 2–8 Years)
Maturity: Silverfish reach sexual maturity in about 3 to 4 months under warm conditions (or up to 2 years in colder environments).
 
Indefinite Molting: Unique among insects, adult silverfish continue to molt throughout their adult lives (up to 50 times in total), shedding their skin periodically to repair damaged limbs or antennas.
Longevity: They are extraordinarily long-lived for household pests, often surviving 3 to 8 years, and can endure up to 300 days without food if water is available.
