HOUSEFLY
The housefly (Musca domestica) is one of the most common insects found worldwide. They are commensal pests, thriving wherever human activity creates organic waste, food refuse, and moisture.
Physical Characteristics
 
Standard housefly physical features
Size & Color: Typically 6 to 7 mm long, with a gray to dark-gray thorax featuring four dark longitudinal stripes.
Mouthparts: Sponging mouthparts (proboscis) designed exclusively for liquid food. Flies secrete saliva onto solid food to liquefy it before sucking it up.
Eyes: Large compound eyes consisting of thousands of individual lenses, providing an almost $360^\circ$ field of vision and rapid motion detection.
Flight: A single pair of membranous wings, with the hind wings modified into small knobbed structures called halteres that act as gyroscopic stabilizers during flight.
2. Life Cycle (Complete Metamorphosis)
 
Four-stage life cycle of the housefly.
Egg (8–12 hours): Females lay batches of 75 to 150 white, rice-shaped eggs directly onto moist organic matter (garbage, manure, decaying vegetation).
Larva / Maggot (3–5 days): Legless, cream-colored larvae hatch and feed aggressively on the decaying medium, molting three times as they grow.
Pupa (3–6 days): Mature larvae crawl to a dry, dark location to form a hard, reddish-brown protective shell (puparium) where they transform into adults.
Adult (2–4 weeks): Adult flies emerge, become sexually mature within 24 hours, and can lay up to 500–1,000 eggs during their short lifetime.
 
Health Risks & Disease Transmission
Houseflies do not bite, but they are dangerous biological vectors for pathogens due to their feeding habits and mobility:
Pathogens Carried: Transmit over 100 pathogens, including bacteria causing typhoid, cholera, dysentery, salmonellosis, and parasitic worms.
Transmission Mechanism: Bacteria stick to the fine hairs on their legs and body pad. They contaminate surfaces by stepping on food, regurgitating digestive juices, and defecating every few minutes.
